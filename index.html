Credit Risk · Fannie Mae Single-Family Loan Performance, 2003Q1

Default Risk by Loan-to-Value

"Business Question: Which borrower and loan risk segments drove the highest default rates in this cohort?"

PostgreSQL 1.4M Loans Claude Project NotebookLM EU AI Act Review

Verified Findings

Every number below is traced to a query against the full 2003Q1 file — nothing here is estimated.

01
SQ1

Loan-to-value is the strongest single factor associated with default in this cohort. Default rate rose from 0.14% at LTV ≤60 to 3.94% at LTV 90–97 — a ~28x spread, climbing step by step across every bucket in between.

02
SQ2

Credit score confirms the same pattern independently: default rate fell from 2.69% in the weakest score decile to 0.18% in the strongest, a ~15x spread.

03
SQ3

Debt-to-income shows a real but weaker signal: average maximum delinquency status rose from 0.32 (DTI under 20) to 0.76 (DTI 50+) — directional support, not a strong driver on its own.

04
SQ4

Geography is a downstream cost, not a predictor. Once loans had already defaulted, Michigan, Ohio, Florida, Georgia, and Illinois carried the highest total dollar exposure — where losses land, not who defaults.
